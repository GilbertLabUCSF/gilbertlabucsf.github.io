# Research

The Gilbert Lab focuses on synthetic biology, functional genomics, and cancer biology. We study drug resistance in prostate cancer, hematopoietic malignancies, and lung cancer using human cell models and mouse systems.

## Epigenome and Transcriptome Editing with CRISPR

We use nuclease-inactivated CRISPR/dCas9 as an RNA-guided DNA binding platform to recruit enzyme activities or protein complexes to specific target sites. Using CRISPRa (activation) and CRISPRi (inhibition), we control gene expression of protein-coding and non-coding genes, enabling mechanistic studies of how epigenetic states influence cancer cell behavior.

## CRISPRi/a Functional Genomics

We developed genome-scale sgRNA libraries targeting human and mouse genes. Our screens reveal complementary principles of oncogene and non-oncogene addictions to identify genes controlling drug response and cancer properties like metastasis and developmental plasticity.

## Genetic Interaction Mapping

We build genetic interaction maps through pairwise perturbations measuring gene epistasis relationships to identify synthetic lethal interactions, map signaling networks, and characterize poorly understood human genes.
